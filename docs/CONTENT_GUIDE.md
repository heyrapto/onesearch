# OneSearch — Comprehensive Content Guide & Editorial Standards

> The definitive editorial constitution, voice guidelines, failure autopsy frameworks, and content data schemas for OneSearch sector dossiers.

---

## Editorial Mission & Voice Principles

OneSearch is an intelligence platform for builders, written by researchers and seasoned operators. Our content must never read like marketing copy, superficial venture capital press releases, or generic AI-generated blog posts. Every sentence must deliver high informational density, empirical precision, and strategic utility.

We adhere to four core editorial principles:

1. **Forensic Intellectual Honesty:** We analyze business closures without mockery, but with complete unvarnished truth. We do not use euphemisms like *"the company faced strategic headwinds"* when the reality is *"the company ran out of money because uncollateralized loan defaults reached 40% and card debits failed."*
2. **Empirical Ground-Truth Over Hype:** We privilege audited financials, central bank circulars, physical market observations, and academic studies over vanity metrics like Gross Merchandise Value (GMV), registered user counts, or funding announcements.
3. **Respect for the Entrepreneur:** Building in Africa is among the most difficult economic endeavors on Earth. We critique business models, unit economics, and governance structures, never the integrity or bravery of the founders who attempted them.
4. **Radical Brevity & High Signal Density:** We avoid corporate filler phrases (*"in today's rapidly evolving ecosystem"*). We get straight to the facts: costs, margins, regulations, and failure modes.

## 1. Tone, Style & Lexicon Standards

### The OneSearch Voice:
- **Authoritative, not Academic:** We cite peer-reviewed economic studies, but we write in crisp, direct language that a software engineer or product manager can absorb in 5 minutes.
- **Pragmatic, not Cynical:** We document failure modes not to discourage builders, but to insulate them against avoidable death traps.
- **Locally Rooted, Globally Lucid:** We use authentic Nigerian business terminology (*"agbero levies"*, *"market women"*, *"POS terminals"*, *"Japa syndrome"*, *"NIBSS instant payment"*, *"parallel market FX"*) while explaining the structural economic mechanics so diaspora or international readers understand the root cause.

## 4. The 7-Part Sector Dossier Framework

Every complete sector dossier must adhere strictly to the following 7-part structure:

### Part 1: Macro Horizon & Structural Dynamics
- What role does this sector play in the broader West African economy?
- How has currency devaluation (floating Naira) and fuel inflation affected the operating environment?
- What are the true solvent customer pool demographics?

### Part 2: The Hall of Winners (Who Cracked the Code)
- Exactly which companies are profitable or operationally sustainable?
- What is their core moat? (Physical hardware terminals? B2B enterprise contracts? Captive off-take agreements?)
- What did they do differently from their peers who collapsed?

### Part 3: The Forensic Graveyard (Why Past Ventures Died)
- Step-by-step dissection of 2 to 4 prominent startups that crashed.
- Exact dates, funding amounts, and the fatal structural miscalculation.

### Part 4: The Unit Economics Teardown
- Exact cost breakdown of delivering one unit of value (one delivery, one POS terminal, one patient consult, one software subscription).
- Gross margin percentage after deducting server hosting, payment gateway fees, and local logistics.
- The break-even customer volume required for survival.

### Part 5: Sovereign Regulatory & Legal Landmines
- Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) licensing tiers and capital deposit requirements.
- State-level transport union levies, local council taxes, and informal extortion vectors.
- Consumer protection regulations (FCCPC) and data privacy penalties (NDPC).

### Part 6: Unexploited High-Conviction Market Gaps
- Specific, underserved customer segments where customers are currently losing money and are desperate for a solution.
- The recommended product wedge and monetization model.

### Part 7: The Founder's Tactical Checklist
- 5 specific pre-launch verification questions the founder must answer before writing code or incorporating an entity.

## 5. The Forensic Failure Autopsy Blueprint

When documenting a startup failure, writers must use the standardized **Four-Stage Autopsy Template**:

```markdown
### Case Study: [Company Name]
- **Operational Timeline:** [Launch Year] – [Shutdown / Restructuring Year]
- **Total Capital Consumed:** $[X] Million (Seed, Series A, Venture Debt)
- **Primary Business Model:** [1-sentence description of what they sold and to whom]
- **The Initial Promise:** [Why investors and founders believed it would scale]
- **The Fatal Flaw (Root Cause):** [The structural economic, biological, or regulatory barrier that broke the model]
- **The Liquidity Collapse:** [The exact sequence of events that triggered insolvency: e.g., default wave, currency crash, regulatory decree]
- **The Strategic Lesson for Future Builders:** [The permanent takeaway for founders entering this sector]
```

### Example Failure Autopsy:
> **Company:** Farmcrowdy  
> **Timeline:** 2016 – 2021  
> **Capital Consumed:** ~$2.5M+ (Techstars, local venture funds)  
> **Primary Business Model:** Crowdfunded agricultural sponsorship promising retail urban investors 15%–25% returns in 6 months to fund poultry and cassava farms.  
> **The Fatal Flaw:** Biological and weather covariance risk cannot support guaranteed fixed returns. Poultry mortality from viral epidemics and transportation delays caused severe yield losses that wiped out reserve margins.  
> **The Collapse:** When harvests underperformed and off-takers delayed payments, the platform lacked cash reserves to repay maturing retail sponsorships. Attempting to use new incoming sponsorships to pay maturing cycles created an unsustainable liquidity squeeze exacerbated by COVID supply chain disruptions.  
> **The Lesson:** Never promise fixed financial returns on volatile biological agricultural assets without institutional parametric weather insurance.

## 8. The Unexploited Market Gap Framework

Market gaps must pass the **Solvent Demand Filter**:

1. **Who is the buyer?** Must be a clearly identified economic actor with verified daily or weekly cash income (e.g., wholesale medicine distributors, corporate HR directors, school proprietors).
2. **What is the current alternative?** How are they solving the problem right now? (Paper notebooks, phone calls, physical cash couriers).
3. **Why will they pay?** The solution must either (a) directly increase their daily cash revenue, or (b) directly reduce their monthly operational expenses by at least 30%. Solutions that offer "convenience" or "lifestyle enhancement" are rejected.

## 13. Deep Dive Examples: Standardized Sector Autopsy Models

To provide writers with absolute clarity, the following reference autopsies demonstrate the exact depth, tone, and empirical rigor expected in every sector dossier:

### Model Autopsy 1: 54gene (Healthtech / Biotechnology)
- **Operational Timeline:** 2019 – 2023
- **Capital Consumed:** $45+ Million (Y Combinator, Adjuvant Capital, Novastar Ventures)
- **Primary Business Model:** Pan-African human genomic biobank collecting DNA samples for pharmaceutical drug discovery and diagnostic clinical testing.
- **The Initial Promise:** Less than 3% of genetic material used in global medical research was African, despite Africans having the highest human genetic diversity. 54gene promised to aggregate proprietary genetic data and license it to global pharma giants like Novartis and Pfizer for billions of dollars.
- **The Fatal Flaw (Root Cause):** Genomic research is an ultra-long-horizon science requiring 7 to 12 years before commercial drug royalties materialize. During COVID-19, 54gene pivoted into rapid mobile COVID lab testing, generating tens of millions of dollars in windfall revenue. Instead of husbanding this cash, management treated temporary COVID testing revenues as permanent SaaS ARR, hiring hundreds of staff, expanding expensive lab infrastructure, and procuring multi-million-dollar sequencing robotics requiring imported reagents priced in USD.
- **The Liquidity Collapse:** When international travel restrictions and mandatory COVID PCR testing ended in 2022, test revenues collapsed by over 90% in 60 days. The core biobank licensing business was years away from generating cash flow. High fixed lab overheads, coupled with leadership governance disputes, multiple CEO resignations, and lawsuits from former executives, made further venture fundraising impossible. The company liquidated its lab equipment and ceased operations in late 2023.
- **The Strategic Lesson for Future Builders:** Never mistake a temporary windfall pandemic subsidy for a permanent customer willingness-to-pay. In capital-heavy science and hardware plays, recurring overheads denominated in USD will destroy you the moment discretionary revenues normalize.

---

### Model Autopsy 2: Jumia Food (Food & On-Demand Quick Commerce)
- **Operational Timeline:** 2013 – December 2023
- **Capital Consumed:** Part of Jumia Group's $1+ Billion cumulative venture burn
- **Primary Business Model:** On-demand restaurant meal delivery marketplace connecting urban consumers with restaurants via motorbike couriers in Nigeria, Kenya, Morocco, Ivory Coast, Tunisia, Uganda, and Ghana.
- **The Initial Promise:** Rapid urbanization, smartphone proliferation, and a rising young middle class would lead to millions of daily meal deliveries, creating a dominant consumer super-app moat.
- **The Fatal Flaw (Root Cause):** Low geographic order density combined with extreme traffic congestion. In Lagos, dispatch riders spent 45 to 75 minutes navigating gridlock to deliver a single ₦4,000 ($2.50) meal. Jumia Food took a 15%–20% restaurant commission (₦600–₦800) while spending over ₦1,200 on courier fuel, dispatch pay, and packaging subsidies. Every delivered meal lost money on a net contribution basis.
- **The Liquidity Collapse:** When Jumia listed on the NYSE and came under pressure from public market investors to achieve profitability, it could no longer justify multi-million-dollar annual cash burn on food delivery. The 2023 removal of Nigeria's petrol subsidy quadrupled motorbike running costs overnight, turning an already difficult unit-economic model into a financial catastrophe. Jumia announced the total shutdown of Jumia Food across all African markets in December 2023.
- **The Strategic Lesson for Future Builders:** You cannot subsidize unit-level logistics in low-density, low-basket-value emerging markets. Food delivery only survives through hyper-local clustering (under 3km radius), batch routing, and charging 100% cost-reflective delivery fees to affluent customers from Day 1.

---

### Model Autopsy 3: Sendy (Logistics & Freight Marketplace)
- **Operational Timeline:** 2015 – 2023
- **Capital Consumed:** ~$29 Million (Toyota Tsusho, Atlantica Ventures, Mobility 54)
- **Primary Business Model:** Asset-light digital logistics marketplace connecting package senders and B2B retailers with vans, trucks, and motorbikes in Kenya, Nigeria, Uganda, and Côte d'Ivoire.
- **The Initial Promise:** Eliminate middlemen in the highly fragmented road freight and dispatch market through mobile app dispatch and algorithmic pricing.
- **The Fatal Flaw (Root Cause):** The pivot into "Sendy Supply" (buying inventory from FMCG manufacturers and reselling to informal roadside retailers with free delivery). Sendy took on working capital risk, inventory holding costs, and fleet depreciation in an environment where FMCG gross margins were razor-thin (3%–5%) and fuel inflation was escalating.
- **The Liquidity Collapse:** High burn rates of over $500,000 monthly were sustainable during the 2020–2021 era of zero interest rates. When global tech funding dried up in mid-2022, Sendy attempted an emergency bridge round that fell through. After a desperate effort to find an institutional trade acquirer failed, the company entered formal liquidation in late 2023, selling off its software IP.
- **The Strategic Lesson for Future Builders:** Do not transition from a high-margin software broker into a low-margin inventory holder unless you have permanent, non-venture working capital debt facilities. Low-margin wholesale retail combined with expensive logistics kills tech companies.

## 16. Technical Markdown & Formatting Standards

To ensure that the Next.js markdown rendering engine (`react-markdown` or native server component renderer) parses and displays content flawlessly, authors must adhere to the following markdown formatting standards:

### 1. Alert Boxes & Callouts
Use GitHub-style alert callouts to highlight critical founder warnings and regulatory caveats:
```markdown
> [!WARNING]
> Regulatory Landmine: Transport unions in Lagos collect daily physical cash tickets from dispatch motorbikes regardless of state courier licenses.

> [!TIP]
> Operational Moat: Always require upfront card payment or bank transfer before dispatching physical inventory to eliminate cash-on-delivery cancellation losses.
```

### 2. Table Formatting
Tables must always include explicit column header alignments and avoid unescaped pipe characters:
```markdown
| Sector Metric | Low Estimate | High Estimate | Verification Source |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---|
| Median Basket Size | ₦3,500 | ₦8,000 | Consumer Retail Audit 2024 |
```

### 3. Financial Currency Formatting
- Standard Nigerian currency must always be formatted with the Naira symbol `₦`, separated by commas, e.g., `₦2,000`, `₦50,000`, `₦1,200,000`.
- For foreign exchange comparisons, explicitly cite the exchange rate assumption used, e.g., `($1.30 USD at ₦1,500 / $1)`.
